← Ration Forecasts

This page explains how the budget headings Dairy Concentrates and Youngstock Concentrates are calculated. Both sit under Purchases, Bought Feed, on the financial budget. Each farm (CM and GAD) and each month is calculated on its own. The fiscal year runs from April to March.

The September 2026 figures later on this page were read from the live dashboard on 21 September 2026. They are a worked example of that month. Later changes to heads, dry cow percentage, or ingredient prices will change a refill of the budget.

1. Monthly amount

Every line uses the same shape:

£ = average heads × herd factor × concentrate £/head/day × days in the month

Dairy concentrate is the milker, far off, and close up lines added together. Youngstock concentrate is the calf, pre-buller, buller, and pregnant heifer lines added together. Each total is rounded to the nearest pound. A line with no ration rate is left out of the sum. The rest of the month is still calculated.

The same head factors are used for forage and straw. Those two budget lines store dairy and youngstock as one combined total. Concentrates stay split.

2. Concentrate rate (£/head/day)

Ingredient prices are entered once, in £ per tonne, on the Ingredients page. Kilograms per head per day are entered on each farm ration (CM and GAD).

For every concentrate ingredient on the ration:

£/head/day = (kg/head/day ÷ 1,000) × £/tonne

Those amounts are added and kept to 4 decimal places. Forage and straw on the same ration are ignored for these two budget lines.

3. Which rations count

The ration name must be the farm code, a space, then an exact group name. The groups used for concentrates are:

Group Accepted names Example
Milkers Milkers, Milker CM Milkers
Far off Far Off, FarOff GAD Far Off
Close up Close Up, CloseUp CM Close Up
Bullers Bullers, Bulling Heifer, Bulling GAD Bullers

Any other name is skipped for this calculation. Calf, pre-buller, and pregnant heifer lines do not have their own rations. They reuse the milkers, bullers, or far off rate, as set out below. If two rations match the same group, the later one is used.

4. Kilograms and prices

  • A blank kilogram cell keeps the last entered kg/head for that ingredient, including from before the fiscal year.
  • The £/tonne has to be entered for that month. A price from another month is not reused.
  • If any concentrate ingredient has kilograms and no price, that ration’s concentrate rate for the month is blank, and that line drops out of the month.

5. Heads and dry cows

Heads come from the stock forecast. Months up to last month use actual herd movements. The current month onwards is projected. Average heads are opening plus closing, divided by two.

Input How it is taken Used for
Average dairy cows (opening Dairy + closing Dairy) ÷ 2 Milkers, far off, close up
Average youngstock + beef (opening and closing Youngstock, plus opening and closing Beef) ÷ 2 Calf, pre-bullers, bullers, pregnant heifers
Dry cow % Dry Cows (%) on Livestock Forecasts, for that farm and month. A missing month is treated as 0%. Split of the dairy herd
Days Length of the calendar month (28, 29, 30, or 31) Every line

Dairy heads are cows with a lactation number above zero. Youngstock are dairy heifers that have not yet calved. Beef is the rest of the herd. There is no separate calf or heifer count inside the concentrate calculation. The youngstock lines are fixed shares of the combined youngstock and beef average.

6. Dairy concentrates

Dry cows are split in half between far off and close up. The rest of the average dairy herd is on the milkers ration. At 12% dry, that is 88% milkers, 6% far off, and 6% close up.

Line Heads Factor Rate
Milkers Average dairy cows 1 − dry % Milkers concentrate
Far off Average dairy cows dry % × 0.5 Far off concentrate
Close up Average dairy cows dry % × 0.5 Close up concentrate

Dairy £ = round(milkers + far off + close up)

7. Youngstock concentrates

These lines are fixed shares of the combined youngstock and beef average. Calf and pre-bullers are priced on the milkers concentrate rate. Bullers use the bullers ration. Pregnant heifers use 75% of the far off rate.

Line What the factor means Factor Rate
Calf 12.5% of heads on the full milkers rate 0.125 Milkers
Pre-bullers 33.33% of heads, eating 18% of the milkers rate 0.0600 Milkers
Bullers 15% of heads on the bullers ration 0.1500 Bullers
Pregnant heifers 15% of heads, eating 75% of the far off rate 0.1125 Far off

Together those factors cover about 44.75% of youngstock and beef head-days. The other 55% has no concentrate cost in this model.

Youngstock £ = round(calf + pre-bullers + bullers + pregnant heifers)

8. Rounding and the budget

The budget stores the rounded sum of the unrounded line costs. Half-pounds round to the nearest even pound, so £6,308.50 becomes £6,308. On the feed purchase forecast, the line-item columns are rounded one by one after that total is taken, so those columns can differ from the budget figure by £1.

Filling the financial budget from data sources writes these pounds onto any heading mapped to dairy concentrates or youngstock concentrates. CM and GAD are stored separately. Replace overwrites a cell that already has a figure. Fill empty leaves a cell that already has a figure. A heading mapped to more than one source adds those amounts and rounds again.
